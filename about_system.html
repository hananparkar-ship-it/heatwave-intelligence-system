Understand heat risk

Heatwave Information

Heatwaves are assessed from high daily maximum temperatures in relation to local climate. Humidity, how long heat lasts and overnight conditions also shape the strain people experience.

Understanding Heatwaves

What is a heatwave?

A heatwave is a period of unusually high temperature that can affect health and daily life. Its assessment depends on observations from a specific location and the usual climate there; a broad zone name alone cannot determine risk.

How heatwaves are identified

IMD criteria primarily consider a station’s daily maximum temperature and its departure from normal. The reference thresholds below vary for plains, hills and coastal stations.

Threshold for considering a heatwave
Maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C in plains or 30°C in hilly regions.
Heatwave
Departure of 4.5–6.4°C from normal, or actual maximum temperature of at least 45°C.
Severe heatwave
Departure of at least 6.5°C from normal, or actual maximum temperature of at least 47°C.
Coastal stations
Maximum temperature of at least 37°C with a departure of at least 4.5°C.

Criteria summary: India Meteorological Department heatwave guidance.

Humidity and duration

Humidity can make it harder for the body to cool itself, while repeated hot days and warm nights can limit recovery. These conditions affect heat stress, but humidity alone is not an IMD heatwave classification criterion.

During extreme heat

Plan outdoor activities for cooler hours, drink water regularly and follow local health and weather advisories. Seek medical help promptly if someone shows signs of serious heat illness.

Heat Safety

  • Stay out of peak heat. Schedule essential outdoor activity for cooler hours and use shade.
  • Stay hydrated. Drink water regularly and follow health advice about fluid intake.
  • Keep your home cool. Close blinds during direct sun and use a cooler room where possible.
  • Avoid strenuous activity during the hottest part of the day; take frequent breaks if work outdoors cannot be postponed.
  • Check on people at greater risk, including older adults, young children and people with health conditions.
  • Know the warning signs. Confusion, collapse or severe weakness needs urgent medical attention.

More guidance: IMD heat-safety advice and National Centre for Disease Control heat-health advisory.
