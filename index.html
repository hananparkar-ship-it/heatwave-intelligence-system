01

Understand the readings

Heatwave Monitoring

Temperature, humidity and forecast information help describe heat conditions and possible stress on people.

Regional summaries and hotspot views organize this information so residents and planning teams can find relevant guidance quickly. Use official weather updates for current readings and decisions.

02

Know when risk rises

Heatwave Alerts

Heat alerts communicate warning levels, affected areas and recommended precautions when heat risk rises. The alerts page links to official sources for current warnings.

Early warnings help people plan daily activities, reduce unnecessary heat exposure and take suitable safety measures. Authorities can use alerts to coordinate local responses.

03

Explore affected areas

Hotspot Information

The hotspot map provides a clear route to regional summaries and helps visitors locate the relevant zone.

For current affected locations, review official warnings before travelling, working outdoors or planning outdoor activities.

04

Reduce heat exposure

Safety Advisory

During a heatwave, drink plenty of water and avoid unnecessary outdoor activities during peak afternoon hours. Staying indoors or in shaded areas can reduce exposure to extreme heat.

Wear light, comfortable clothing, take regular breaks when working outdoors and keep homes properly ventilated during periods of high temperature.

05

Get help quickly

Emergency Information

Seek medical assistance immediately for heat-related illness. Warning signs may include dizziness, weakness, excessive sweating and confusion.

Move the person to a cooler location and avoid further exposure to heat. Monitor older adults, children and people working outdoors carefully during extreme heat.
